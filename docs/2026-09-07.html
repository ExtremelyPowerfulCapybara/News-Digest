Venta masiva de bonos globales aviva el fantasma de inflación estructural persistente
Los rendimientos del bono del Tesoro estadounidense a 10 años alcanzaron su nivel más alto desde noviembre de 2023, mientras que los gilts británicos tocaron máximos desde 2008 y el bono japonés a 10 años superó el 3% por primera vez desde 1996. Gestores de fondos advierten que la combinación de proteccionismo comercial, gasto en defensa y la guerra en Medio Oriente ha desplazado el régimen macroeconómico hacia una inflación estructuralmente más alta, con implicaciones de largo alcance para las carteras de inversión.
Esta semana el discurso de Kevin Warsh en Jackson Hole reforzó las expectativas de una subida de tasas el 15-16 de septiembre, lo que hace que la señal de los mercados de bonos sea especialmente relevante para la reunión del FOMC.

Global bond selloff revives the specter of persistent structural inflation
U.S. 10-year Treasury yields hit their highest level since November 2023, U.K. gilts touched post-2008 highs, and Japan's 10-year bond surpassed 3% for the first time since 1996. Fund managers warn that the combination of trade protectionism, defense spending, and the Middle East war has shifted the macroeconomic regime toward structurally higher inflation, with far-reaching implications for investment portfolios.
Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech this week reinforced expectations of a September 15-16 rate hike, making the bond market's signal especially consequential for the upcoming FOMC meeting.

El presidente Trump escaló su campaña de presión sobre la Reserva Federal amenazando con suspender el comercio con países que tengan superávit comercial con EE.UU. a menos que la Fed reduzca las tasas —la primera vez que vincula directamente los aranceles con la política monetaria. Trump threatens tariffs if the Fed doesn't cut rates, intensifying pressure on Warsh
President Trump escalated his pressure campaign on the Federal Reserve by threatening to halt trade with countries running surpluses with the U.S. unless the Fed lowers rates — the first time he has directly linked tariffs to monetary policy. The broadside includes statements from Vice President Vance, Secretary Bessent, and advisor Navarro, who called FOMC members 'clowns,' as markets assign roughly 60% probability to a rate hike at the September 15-16 meeting.
Fed independence is the single most closely watched institutional asset in fixed income markets right now; any sign that Warsh yields to political pressure could accelerate the bond selloff and weaken the dollar.

Los mayores gestores de activos del mundo reconstruyen posiciones en oro
Los principales fondos de gestión de activos a nivel global están incrementando sus tenencias de oro anticipando valor a largo plazo, pese a las medidas antiinflacionarias de la Fed y el entorno de tasas elevadas. El reposicionamiento en oro ocurre simultáneamente con la venta de bonos soberanos globales, sugiriendo que el mercado está recalibrado su concepción de activo refugio de referencia en un régimen de inflación estructuralmente más alta.

World's largest asset managers rebuild gold positions
The world's leading asset management funds are increasing their gold holdings in anticipation of long-term value, despite the Fed's anti-inflation measures and the high-rate environment. The move reflects a deliberate hedge against structural inflation and monetary policy uncertainty, at a time when sovereign bonds are no longer offering their traditional safe-haven appeal.
The repositioning into gold is happening simultaneously with the global sovereign bond selloff, suggesting markets are recalibrating their preferred safe-haven asset under a structurally higher inflation regime.

Argentine bonds break below 500 bps country risk as Merval recovers ground in September
Global bonds opened September with gains of 0.5% to 1.1% and local-law bonds (Bonares) advanced 0.4% to 1.2%, pushing country risk back below 500 basis points after an August in which the Merval plunged 9.2% to $1,896 with no concrete catalysts. The rebound arrives with more depressed valuations and against a backdrop of the U.S. 10-year Treasury hitting 4.8%, creating additional pressure on emerging market debt.
Compressing the country risk below 500 basis points is a key psychological threshold for Argentina's access to voluntary debt markets, a central objective of its current IMF program.
