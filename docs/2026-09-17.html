Rendimientos del bono a 10 años alcanzan máximos desde 2007 ante inminente decisión de la Fed
El rendimiento del bono del Tesoro estadounidense a 10 años tocó su nivel más alto desde 2007, con el mercado asignando un 90% de probabilidad a una subida de tasas en la reunión de la Fed del miércoles. El dato convierte cualquier señal de la Fed sobre el ritmo futuro de alzas — el llamado 'dot plot' — en el verdadero evento de riesgo: una sola subida ya está descontada; lo que mueve mercados es cuántas más vienen.
Con el IPC estadounidense en 3.4% anual a agosto — aún por encima del objetivo del 2% de la Fed — el mercado está empezando a cotizar no una pausa, sino un ciclo de alzas prolongado que podría redefinir las valoraciones de todos los activos.

10-Year Treasury Yields Hit 2007 Highs Ahead of Imminent Fed Decision
The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield touched its highest level since 2007, with the market assigning a 90% probability to a rate hike at Wednesday's Fed meeting. The data makes any Fed signal on the future pace of hikes — the so-called dot plot — the real risk event: a single hike is already priced in; what moves markets is how many more are coming.
With U.S. CPI at 3.4% annually through August — still above the Fed's 2% target — the market is beginning to price not a pause but a prolonged hiking cycle that could reprice all asset classes.

Wall Street's Biggest Fear Is No Longer the AI Bubble — It's a Disorderly Bond Selloff
Bank of America's latest global fund manager survey shows that a 'disorderly rise' in bond yields has displaced the artificial intelligence bubble as the number-one risk identified by investment professionals. Mexico's Treasury Projects 1.8% Growth for 2026, But the Manufacturing Engine May Be Changing Hands
Mexico's SHCP Economic Policy Criteria for the PPEF 2027 maintain a 1.8% growth forecast for Mexico in 2026, sustained by a second-quarter recovery, while the U.S. manufacturing boom reorients toward semiconductors, data centers and artificial intelligence — sectors where Mexico contributes little value added. If the historical elasticity between U.S. and Mexican GDP deteriorates because the investment cycle benefits China and its component supply chains more, the Treasury's optimism may remain just that — optimism.
The analysis arrives as U.S. rates at historic highs raise the cost of capital for investment in Mexico precisely when the country most needs to attract high-value-added tech nearshoring projects.
