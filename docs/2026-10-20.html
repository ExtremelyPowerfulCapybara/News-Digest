Indonesia se hunde a mínimos de seis años en bolsa tras amenaza de MSCI de expulsarla de sus índices emergentes

Indonesian stocks slide to six-year low as MSCI warning of expulsion from emerging markets indexes rattles investors
Jakarta's benchmark stocks fell over 5% in early trading yesterday, compounding a staggering 11.6% plunge on Friday, after MSCI said it was considering shifting Indonesia to 'standalone' status — a group that includes Lebanon and Zimbabwe. Goldman Sachs estimates a full MSCI removal could trigger $5.6 billion in passive outflows, with active funds benchmarked to these indexes holding more than $8 billion, although any selling would be discretionary.
The episode contrasts with the MSCI Emerging Markets index gaining 0.7% this week, suggesting the selloff is Indonesia-specific rather than a broad EM phenomenon; foreign investors had already net sold $4.88 billion in Indonesian stocks year-to-date.

Treasury yields rise as 10-year hits 5.146% ahead of the Fed's October 28th meeting
The yield on the 10-year US Treasury rose more than 4 basis points to 5.146% yesterday, with the 30-year at 5.478%, amid inflation data showing wholesale prices surging 6.3% annually in September — the biggest jump since 2022.
