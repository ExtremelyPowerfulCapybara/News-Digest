Nvidia halts chip shipments to Southeast Asia amid tightening U.S. export controls
Nvidia has instructed manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia — countries that had become a back door for Chinese AI developers seeking access to advanced chips. The move threatens revenues that exceeded $104 billion through Nvidia's Singapore unit alone in 2025, and comes even as Jensen Huang's return to the CEO role has not opened the door to a looser application of the new rules.
Nvidia's move coincides with Trump's new tariff salvo targeting 14 trading partners, several of them in Southeast Asia, adding an extra layer of regulatory and trade uncertainty for the region, according to today's CNBC and CNA articles.

Trump's tariff threats pile pressure on trading partners to sign deals with Washington
The Trump administration proposed tariffs ranging from 15% to 40% on imports from 14 economies, with Vietnam, China and India among the hardest hit, though rates could be capped lower for those that conclude trade agreements with Washington. Vietnam quickly signaled it would work to finalize a deal as soon as possible, while China rejected allegations of excess industrial capacity and demanded the U.S. implement $30 billion in reciprocal tariff cuts agreed in September.
The Supreme Court had already struck down Trump's emergency tariffs in February, making this new Section 301 round a deliberate legal reframing; Capital Economics analysts note in today's article that the net impact may be limited, as it puts many countries back where they were earlier in the year.

Gold slips below $3,500 support before bouncing back as markets price in Fed rate hike at December meeting
Spot gold briefly fell below the critical $3,500 level on Wednesday — its lowest since September 2025 — after October PCE inflation came in at 3.3% year-on-year and third-quarter GDP grew 4.3%, both above expectations. Deutsche Bank: los 'chip-dollars' reemplazan a los petrodólares como ancla del dominio del dólar, pero con mayor riesgo
Según un análisis de Deutsche Bank firmado por la estratega de FX Mallika Sachdeva, el comercio global de chips supera por primera vez al de petróleo y gas, y el superávit comercial en chips de Asia —excluyendo China— iguala ya al de los países del Golfo en hidrocarburos. El nuevo régimen 'chip-dollar' es estructuralmente más inestable que el petrodólar porque depende de las valoraciones bursátiles estadounidenses en lugar de la deuda soberana, y el 70% de los beneficios de la industria de chips fluye hacia empresas de EE.UU. a pesar de que Asia domina la fabricación.
El análisis cobra relevancia directa hoy dado que el DXY se mantiene en 103.79 y el Nasdaq cae un 2.1%, ya que la tesis del 'chip-dollar' implica que una corrección sostenida en acciones tecnológicas de EE.UU. podría erosionar el mecanismo mismo que recicla capital asiático hacia activos estadounidenses.

Deutsche Bank: 'chip-dollars' are replacing petrodollars as the anchor of dollar dominance — but the new regime is 'inherently riskier'
According to Deutsche Bank FX strategist Mallika Sachdeva, global chip trade has surpassed oil and gas trade for the first time in modern history, and Asia's chip-trade surplus — excluding China — now matches the petroleum surpluses of Persian Gulf nations. The new 'chip-dollar' regime is structurally less stable than the petrodollar because it depends on U.S. equity valuations rather than sovereign debt, even as 70% of the chip industry's profits flow to U.S. firms despite Asia's manufacturing dominance.
The analysis is directly relevant today as the DXY holds at 103.79 and the Nasdaq falls 2.1%, since the 'chip-dollar' thesis implies that a sustained correction in U.S. tech stocks could erode the very mechanism that recycles Asian capital into U.S. assets.

Latin America has the copper and energy AI needs, but infrastructure remains the bottleneck
Chile, Argentina and Brazil hold critical resources for the AI expansion — copper, renewable energy and rare earths — but Chilean copper production fell 8.5% in the first nine months of 2026 even as demand grows at 1.6% annually, according to state copper commission Cochilco.
