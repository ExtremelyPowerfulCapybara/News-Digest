Bessent's short-term debt strategy could trigger a fiscal vicious cycle, warns Barry Eichengreen
Economist Barry Eichengreen argues that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's strategy of issuing more short-term bills while buying back long-term debt — set to save roughly $40 billion in debt-servicing costs on over $2 trillion in net issuance — exposes the federal government to a dangerous rollover risk if inflation reaccelerates. If short-term borrowing costs spike due to renewed inflation — from West Asia tensions or a Fed that was too slow to hike — the resulting vicious cycle of higher deficits and rising yields could ultimately force the Fed to monetise debt, an outcome Eichengreen calls 'unhappily inflationary for all concerned.'
The 10-year Treasury yields 5.07% today: if Waller gets his 50bp hike in December, the spread between bills and long bonds that makes Bessent's strategy profitable could compress or invert, accelerating exactly the risk Eichengreen describes.
