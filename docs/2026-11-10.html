The market bloodbath just exposed Wall Street's true fears about U.S. debt, top economist warns
Robin Brooks, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution, argues that last week's market selloff was a 'natural experiment' proving that the rise in Treasury yields reflects a growing fiscal risk premium — a $2 trillion deficit and $40 trillion in debt — rather than the flood of corporate tech debt that some had used as an alibi. The market-implied 10-year yield ten years forward ('10y10y forward') did not fall alongside shorter-dated yields during the episode, cementing his thesis that the market is charging a sovereign risk premium, not merely adjusting growth expectations.

Trump administration's embrace of 'perp' futures sets off risk alarms for Wall Street stalwarts
The CFTC approved crypto perpetual futures on U.S. markets in May and last week extended authorization to single-stock equity perps, triggering lawsuits from CME Group and a consortium including Cboe and Citadel Securities, who argue the regulator has misclassified the products and underestimated their risks in a multitrillion-dollar derivatives market.
