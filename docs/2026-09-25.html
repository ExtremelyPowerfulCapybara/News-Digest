U.S. diesel hits all-time record of $6.31 per gallon; economists warn of cascading price effects across the entire supply chain
Diesel prices in the United States hit a historic high of $6.31 per gallon — $8 in California — driven by the war with Iran and disruption at the Strait of Hormuz, raising freight costs by road and rail across the economy. With inflation already above 3% and the Fed signaling more hikes, a diesel cost shock creates a stagflationary scenario that no interest rate model easily resolves.

El Financiero México
INEGI: 1.6 millones de mexicanos afectados por desplazamiento forzado; cuatro de cada diez hogares dependen de transferencias gubernamentales
La Encuesta Intercensal 2025 del INEGI revela que entre 2020 y 2025 unos 509,900 hogares —que incluyen aproximadamente 1.6 millones de personas— fueron afectados por el desplazamiento de al menos un integrante debido a violencia o inseguridad, mientras la proporción de hogares que reciben ingresos de programas gubernamentales saltó de 25% a 41.4% en el mismo periodo. La combinación de gasto social creciente para sostener ingresos en zonas de alta inseguridad y una base productiva erosionada por el desplazamiento interno configura un círculo fiscal que presionará las finanzas públicas mexicanas en el mediano plazo.
Estos datos llegan en un momento en que la calificación crediticia soberana de México y la percepción de riesgo país están bajo escrutinio; la dependencia creciente de transferencias gubernamentales en un contexto de finanzas públicas ajustadas añade presión sobre el perfil fiscal que los mercados de deuda ya están monitoreando.

INEGI: 1.6 million Mexicans affected by forced displacement; four in ten households now depend on government transfers
INEGI's 2025 Intercensal Survey reveals that between 2020 and 2025, approximately 509,900 households — encompassing roughly 1.6 million people — were affected by the displacement of at least one member due to violence or insecurity, while the share of households receiving income from government programs jumped from 25% to 41.4% in the same period. The combination of rising social spending to sustain incomes in high-insecurity areas and a productive base eroded by internal displacement creates a fiscal feedback loop that will pressure Mexican public finances in the medium term.
These data arrive as Mexico's sovereign credit rating and country risk perception are under scrutiny; growing dependence on government transfers in a context of tight public finances adds pressure on the fiscal profile that debt markets are already monitoring.
