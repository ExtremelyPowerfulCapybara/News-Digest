Estimados humanos, arrancamos la semana con dos déficits fiscales que compiten por su atención: el de EE.UU. cerró su año fiscal en casi dos billones de dólares —un récord histórico en tiempos de expansión y pleno empleo— mientras México sigue acumulando deuda sin que ninguna institución autónoma parezca dispuesta a frenar la inercia. Dear humans, we start the week with two fiscal deficits competing for your attention: the U.S. closed its fiscal year at nearly two trillion dollars in the red — a historic record during a period of expansion and full employment — while Mexico keeps piling on debt with no autonomous institution apparently willing to pump the brakes. Futures are opening without much direction, but bank earnings season and this week's IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok will provide the first serious read on the quarter.

El año fiscal estadounidense cerró el 30 de septiembre con un déficit de 1.99 billones de dólares —6.2% del PIB—, con el pago de intereses de deuda alcanzando un récord de 1.143 billones, ya superior al presupuesto de Defensa. Esta historia adquiere urgencia adicional esta semana porque el FMI presentará su World Economic Outlook el martes en Bangkok, donde la trayectoria fiscal de EE.UU. será inevitablemente el elefante en la sala.

U.S. fiscal deficit closes at nearly $2 trillion, a historic record during an expansion cycle
The U.S. fiscal year ended September 30 with a deficit of $1.99 trillion — 6.2% of GDP — with net interest payments hitting a record $1.143 trillion, already surpassing the Defense budget. If stress factors converge, the deficit could approach 10% of GDP in the early part of the next decade, according to stress projections from various institutions. The Treasury bond market and the dollar will be the first to reflect any loss of confidence in U.S. fiscal sustainability.
This story gains added urgency this week as the IMF releases its World Economic Outlook on Tuesday in Bangkok, where the U.S. fiscal trajectory will inevitably be the elephant in the room.
